# 06 - Naming and Trademark Clearance for a Free Cross-Platform Media App

Research date: 2026-10-19
Scope: a free video player + audio player + image viewer/gallery for Android, iOS, macOS and Windows. Goal: a short (4-8 letter), pronounceable, one-word name that works as a brand, a verb, a suffix base ("X Player", "X Pro") and a code identifier, with low legal risk and an obtainable domain and GitHub org.

> **Not legal advice.** This is a pre-screen to shortlist names before paying for a professional clearance search and an attorney opinion. "Appears free / appears taken" statements are unverified (no WHOIS access).

## 4. Final ranking - top 8

Risk per dimension: **App stores / Trademark / Domains / GitHub / Language / Similarity to big players**. "Overall" weights trademark and app-store conflicts most heavily.

### 1. Fovea  (Low / Medium / Medium / Low / Low / Low - overall **Low-Medium**)

- **Why:** The fovea is the part of the retina that gives the sharpest vision - a precise, slightly nerdy metaphor for a viewer that puts everything in focus. Five letters, two syllables (FOH-vee-uh), spelled the same in every Latin-script language, transliterates cleanly. Nothing in the media category on either store; the only same-name software business is Fovea Solutions (Swiss process-mining), which sells enterprise analytics, not consumer media apps, so coexistence is plausible but is the one thing an attorney must evaluate.
- **Works as a verb/suffix/identifier:** "Fovea Player", "Fovea Pro", `fovea`, `FoveaPlayer`, `libfovea`.
- **Tagline:** "Everything, in focus."
- **Package IDs:** `com.<org>.fovea`, iOS bundle `com.<org>.fovea.player`, Android `com.<org>.fovea`; if you secure `fovea.app`, use `app.fovea.*` for libraries.
- **Domains:** `fovea.com` is a company; `fovea.app` did not resolve (appears free - verify and register immediately); `fovea.io` registered but dead (consider a backorder). Fallbacks: `getfovea.com`, `fovea.media`, `foveaplayer.com`, `usefovea.com`.
- **GitHub:** `fovea` org exists (inactive since 2021) - try `foveaapp` (free), `fovea-player`, or ask GitHub for the dormant name via their name-squatting policy.
- **Watch-outs:** medical-sounding to some ears; a dictionary word, so expect coexisting marks in medical/optical classes (fine, different goods).

### 2. Kuvio  (Low / Low-Medium / High / Low / Low / Low - overall **Medium**, driven by domains)

- **Why:** Finnish *kuvio* = pattern/figure, from *kuva* = picture. Coined-sounding everywhere except Finland, distinctive and highly registrable in class 9. Zero app-store presence. GitHub org `kuvio` is free.
- **Tagline:** "Every picture, every pattern, one player."
- **Package IDs:** `com.<org>.kuvio`, `io.kuvio.*` only if you later acquire kuvio.io (unlikely - it is a live company).
- **Domains:** all three primary TLDs are live businesses (photo studio, web builder, ad-tech). Realistic options: `kuvio.media`, `kuvioapp.com`, `getkuvio.com`, `kuvio.dev`, `kuvio.app` is taken. Alternative spellings if you want the .com: **Kuviyo**, **Kuvio HQ**, **Kuvia**.
- **Watch-outs:** the Finnish photo studio is media-adjacent (class 41 photography) - low practical overlap with a player app but note it in the clearance search; "Kuvio" is also a common Finnish company name element.

### 3. Mediaro  (Low / Medium-Low / Low / Low / Low / Low-Medium - overall **Medium-Low**)

- **Why:** Instantly says "media", the "-aro" suffix makes it suggestive rather than descriptive, so it is registrable while still being self-explanatory in app-store search. No app on either store, GitHub org free, `.app` and `.io` appear unregistered.
- **Tagline:** "All your media. One Mediaro."
- **Package IDs:** `com.<org>.mediaro`, `app.mediaro.*` (if mediaro.app is registered by you).
- **Domains:** `mediaro.com` is for sale on Atom (premium price); `mediaro.app` and `mediaro.io` appear free - register both today. Fallbacks: `mediaro.media`, `getmediaro.com`.
- **Watch-outs:** three syllables plus a generic root means weaker distinctiveness than Fovea/Kuvio; MEDIAROOM (IPTV operator platform) is the closest existing mark - different customers, but list it for the attorney. Hindi/Arabic speakers may read it as "media + ro"; no negative meaning found.

### 4. Vidro  (Low / Medium / Medium-High / Medium / Low / Medium - overall **Medium**)

- **Why:** Portuguese for "glass" - "see your media through clear glass". Five letters, instantly evokes video, no app of this name on either store.
- **Tagline:** "Clear as glass."

## 5. Trademark filing path (2026)

### 5.1 What to file for

- **Nice Class 9** - downloadable software / mobile apps for playing video, audio and images. This is the core class for a free app (you do not need to charge money for a mark to be "used in commerce"; distribution of the app under the mark is use).
- **Class 42** - add if you offer any hosted/online component (SaaS, non-downloadable web player, cloud sync, "providing online non-downloadable software").
- Optional: **Class 41** (entertainment/media content services) only if you curate or host content; **Class 38** if you stream.
- Use the USPTO ID Manual wording verbatim to avoid the custom-identification surcharge.

### 5.2 USPTO (TEAS)

- Base application fee: **$350 per class** (fee rule effective 18 Jan 2025; the USPTO fee page was last updated 18 Jan 2025 and still applies as of this research).
- Surcharges: **$200 per class** for a free-form (custom) goods description, **$100 per class** for insufficient information, **$200** per extra 1,000 characters.
- Intent-to-use follow-ups: **Statement of Use $150 per class**, each six-month **extension $125 per class**.
- Maintenance: Section 8 declaration at year 5-6 **$325 per class** (**$575** combined with Section 15 incontestability); Section 8/9 renewal at year 10 **$650 per class**.
- Typical attorney cost for drafting/filing plus a knock-out search: roughly **$500-2,000 per application**; a full professional clearance search (federal, state, common-law, domains, social) typically **$300-1,500** per mark. Budget for at least one Office Action response (often $300-1,500). Timeline: ~12-18 months to registration if unopposed.
- Foreign-domiciled applicants **must** use a US-licensed attorney.

### 5.3 The "intent to use" (ITU, Section 1(b)) concept

A US application can be filed before the mark is used, as long as you have a **bona fide intention to use it in commerce**. Your sworn statement is normally enough, but keep contemporaneous evidence (design files, store listings in progress, domain purchases, build pipelines) dated consistently with the filing. The filing date becomes your nationwide priority date, which is the whole point for a pre-launch app. After the application is allowed you receive a **Notice of Allowance**; you then have **6 months** to file a Statement of Use (screenshots of the app listing showing the mark), extendable in six-month steps up to **36 months** total, each extension requiring a fee and (after the first) "good cause". Miss the deadline and the application is abandoned (revivable within two months for a fee). Rule of thumb: file ITU the week you commit to the name, ship under that name within a year, then file the Statement of Use.

### 5.4 EUIPO (EU trade mark)

One filing covers all 27 EU member states. Official fees (from EUIPO's published schedule; the EUIPO fee page blocked automated retrieval, so verify on euipo.europa.eu): **EUR 850** for one class filed online, **EUR 50** for the second class, **EUR 150** for each class from the third onward; renewal mirrors the filing fees. No use requirement at filing, but the mark becomes vulnerable to cancellation if not genuinely used within five years. EUIPO examines absolute grounds only (descriptiveness, etc.), not prior marks - prior-rights owners must oppose within three months of publication, so your own TMview search is essential. Expect **EUR 1,000-2,500** all-in with counsel for one or two classes.

### 5.5 Madrid Protocol (WIPO)

File one international application through your home office (USPTO or EUIPO) based on your home application/registration and designate the countries you want. Fees in Swiss francs: a **basic fee of CHF 653** (black-and-white) or **CHF 903** (colour), plus either a complementary fee (CHF 100) per designated country or that country's **individual fee** - for example WIPO lists **CHF 460 per class** for a US designation and **CHF 400 for three classes** for Switzerland. Use the WIPO fee calculator (madrid.wipo.int/feecalcapp) for an exact figure. Caveat: for five years the international registration depends on the home mark ("central attack"). Practical path for a free indie app: **file USPTO ITU first** (cheapest priority date), then within the **6-month Paris Convention priority window** decide whether an EUTM and a Madrid extension (UK, Japan, India, Brazil, China are common picks) are worth it. Many free projects file only US + EU and rely on common-law/unregistered rights elsewhere until there is revenue or a dispute.

### 5.6 Other practical rights

- UK is no longer covered by the EUTM; UK IPO fees are modest (approx. GBP 170 for one class online, GBP 50 per additional class - verify).
- Use **TM** from day one; use **(R)** only after registration in that jurisdiction.
- Register the name as a brand on Amazon/YouTube/social handles early; squatters watch App Store "coming soon" listings.

## 6. Reserve the app names early (and the store rules that bite)

- **Apple App Store Connect.** App names are globally unique per localization across the whole store and limited to **30 characters** (Review Guideline 2.3.7). Creating the app record reserves the name before any build is uploaded; if another developer already holds the name and you have trademark rights, Apple runs a formal app-name dispute process (apple.com/legal/internet-services/itunes/appnamenotices). Apple has historically released names from records that never received a submitted build after a set period (commonly cited as 180 days) - check the current App Store Connect Help page "Add a new app" before relying on a reservation. Create the record the day you choose the name; upload at least a TestFlight build to keep it.
- **Apple's rule against misleading names.** Guideline 2.3.7: choose a unique name, do not pack metadata with "trademarked terms, popular app names" or irrelevant phrases; 4.1 (copycats): you cannot use another developer's "icon, brand, or product name in your app's icon or name" without approval, and impersonating another app can get you expelled from the Developer Program; 5.2.1: no "misleading, false, or copycat representations, names, or metadata". So "Fovea - VLC & Plex Player" would be rejected; "Fovea Player" is fine, and third-party names belong only in the description as factual compatibility statements ("plays files from Plex and Jellyfin servers").
- **Google Play Console.** Titles are also capped at **30 characters**, may not use emoji, all-caps, ranking claims, promotional words, or other brands' logos/celebrity names without permission (Metadata policy). Titles are **not** unique on Play - what is unique and permanent is the **package name** (`com.<org>.<name>`), which can never be changed or reused once an app is published under it. Create the app and push an internal-test build to lock the package name.
- **Microsoft Partner Center.** You explicitly **reserve** a product name; reservations expire if you do not submit within the reservation window (Microsoft documents a three-month window - verify when reserving). Name reservation is free and should be done the same day as the Apple record.
- Keep all three store names, the GitHub org, the domain and the trademark application identical in spelling and casing - inconsistency weakens later trademark enforcement and store disputes.

## 7. Open-source project name vs. trademark

Open-source licences (GPL, MIT, AGPL) license the **code**, never the **name or logo**. The name is the only thing you can keep exclusive while giving the code away, so mature projects protect it deliberately:

- **VideoLAN / VLC.** VideoLAN (a French non-profit) registered VLC, VideoLAN and the traffic-cone logo. The code is GPL/LGPL, but the legal page makes clear that redistributing modified builds under the VLC name or logo, or implying endorsement, requires permission. VideoLAN has used these marks to remove paid "VLC" clones from app stores and to challenge impostor builds - a textbook case of trademark doing the job copyright cannot. (VideoLAN's legal page could not be fetched during this research; statement based on the project's published policy.)
- **Mozilla.** Mozilla's trademark policy says it plainly: because anyone can modify the code, the marks are the only signal that Mozilla stands behind a build. You may say your software is "based on Mozilla technology" or "works with Firefox", with a disclaimer of equal prominence, but you may not ship a user-facing product called "Something-fox" or "Moz-Something", put the marks in a domain, or alter the logos. Distributors of modified builds must rebrand (Debian's historical "Iceweasel" is the famous outcome).
- **Immich.** Immich's README states only AGPLv3 and contains no trademark clause; since 2024 the project has been developed under the FUTO organisation, and brand control effectively sits with that entity rather than in the licence (FUTO's trademark-policy page returned 404 during this research - confirm their current policy). Lesson: an AGPL licence does not stop someone shipping "Immich Pro" on a store; only a trademark (and a policy telling people what is allowed) does.
- Other useful models: the **Kodi** project (XBMC Foundation) registered KODI and publishes a trademark policy after years of "XBMC" boxes being sold with piracy add-ons; the **Rust Foundation** and **Python Software Foundation** publish detailed policies on how community projects may use the name.

### How to write a trademark policy for a free app (1-2 pages)

1. **Identify the marks:** word mark "Fovea", the logo, and the combination; state the owner (you, your company, or a foundation) and that the open-source licence grants no trademark rights.
2. **Allowed without asking:** referring to the app by name in text (nominative use), "works with Fovea" / "plugin for Fovea" phrasing, linking to official downloads, unmodified official builds redistributed with attribution, community meetups and tutorials, academic use.
3. **Allowed with conditions:** forks and modified builds must either remove the name/logo or add a clear "unofficial build, not endorsed by the Fovea project" notice; package repositories (F-Droid, Homebrew, winget, AUR) may use the name for unmodified or lightly patched builds - say so explicitly so distros do not have to ask.
4. **Not allowed:** selling or listing modified versions under the same name on any app store, domains or social handles that begin with the mark, merchandise, implying endorsement, combining the mark with other words to form a new product name ("FoveaTube"), altering the logo.
5. **Enforcement and contact:** an email address, a promise of good-faith resolution, and a note that you will file store takedowns for impersonating listings.
6. **Base it on a template:** the Model Trademark Guidelines (modeltrademarkguidelines.org) and Mozilla's policy are permissively licensed starting points. Publish it as `TRADEMARK.md` next to `LICENSE`, link it from the README and the website, and reference it in the store listing's legal section.
7. **Back it with a registration** in at least the US (and ideally EU) - a policy without a registration is a request, not a right - and keep evidence of continuous use (store listings, release notes, screenshots) for the Statement of Use and later renewals.
